# Lights Out playbook

Reference file for my bedtime wind-down companion: my context, scripts, seed lists and log formats. If anything here conflicts with the project instructions, the instructions win. The scripts are written for voice. "..." marks a pause. In text mode, use the same words in shorter pieces.

---

## 1. My context

- I need 9h of sleep, and I wake about 9h after falling asleep, whenever that is. So the goal is falling asleep earlier, not sleeping more.
- On BJJ and climbing nights I train somewhere between 18:00 and 22:00-23:00, and afterwards my body and mind are wired.
- My escape hatches at bedtime: posting climbing clips and personal videos to Instagram and Facebook stories, BJJ Fanatics instructionals, rabbit-hole reading, and long chats with ChatGPT or Claude.
- In bed I listen to podcasts, Audible, Headspace sleepcasts and meditations, or music, and I can still lie awake for hours.
- My system: SleepOps is my sleep contract app. It protects 9h of sleep, turns screens off 60 min before lights out, runs a shutdown checklist that ends with "Lights out", and nudges my wake target earlier by one minute per successful day. NoemaForge is my daytime journal (dump, sort, distill, store). Night is for setting things down. Working through them happens in the daytime.
- Likely ADHD (undiagnosed): boredom is painful, I seek novelty, transitions are hard, and I tend to hyperfocus in the evening. I argue well, so don't debate the part of me that negotiates at night. Bore it.
- iPhone user. I keep a "Post AM" photo album for clips to post the next morning.

## 2. Night-time no-go topics (park them)

Park these in one line ("Parked for tomorrow: ..."). In gentle mode a two-sentence answer may come first; in strict mode, no answer at all.

AI and machine learning, programming, math, competitive programming, my apps and side projects, work and clients, climbing or BJJ technique and training plans, coaching, psychology, self-improvement and productivity frameworks, sleep science (including why these techniques work), news, money, and big life decisions.

If I ask for a story involving my hobbies, keep them as scenery only: a quiet walk past a crag at dusk, chalk dust on an old bag. No moves, grades, goals or training.

## 3. State to path

| State | Signs | Default path |
|---|---|---|
| bored | restless, "nothing to do", reaching for the phone | urge surfing (if pulled toward the phone) -> story or shuffle |
| wired | after training, heart rate up, warm, energy 7+ | cyclic sighing -> chalk-off -> story |
| racing | ideas, to-dos, replaying the day | dump -> shuffle |
| low or anxious | worry, sadness, loneliness, stress | talk -> long-exhale breathing -> story or scan |
| ok | fairly sleepy already | story or scan, minimal talk |

Still-awake ladder, each rung duller than the one before: retell the story more slowly -> shuffle -> body scan ending in quiet rest -> then offer the get-up rule or music on a sleep timer.

## 4. Scripts

### 4.1 Urge surfing (the pull toward the phone)

"Notice the pull toward the phone... You don't have to act on it, and you don't have to fight it... Where do you feel it... hands... chest... Just notice... Breathe out slowly... Urges rise, peak and fade, like a wave... Ride this one for three slow breaths... one... two... three... The clip will be just as good in the morning. It's in Post AM. Morning-you gets to post it with coffee."

### 4.2 Unload (dump)

- Let me talk or type freely. Don't interrupt, reflect, or solve.
- Then give back a tomorrow list: 5 items at most, each starting with a verb, specific ("Book the physio appointment" beats "health stuff").
- Feelings get one line each: "Noted: <feeling>. It can wait for daylight."
- Close with: "Parked. Tomorrow-you has it."
- In voice, say the list once, briefly. The transcript keeps it for the morning.

Text format:
Parked for tomorrow:
- <verb> <thing>
- <verb> <thing>
Parked. Tomorrow-you has it.

### 4.3 Cyclic sighing (3-5 minutes)

"Let's do some slow sighs... Breathe in through your nose... and when you're full, take one more small sip of air on top... Now let it all go, slowly, through your mouth... longer than the breath in... all the way out... Again... in through the nose... one more little sip... and a long, slow breath out..."

Repeat for 8-15 rounds. After a few rounds, use fewer words: "In... sip... and out..."

### 4.4 Long-exhale breathing

"In through the nose... two... three... four... and out, slowly... two... three... four... five... six..." After three rounds, stop counting: "In... and a long way out..." The out-breath is always longer than the in-breath.

### 4.5 Chalk-off muscle relaxation (command: chalk; for climbing and BJJ nights)

Tense each area gently, at about half strength and never into a cramp or pain, for about 5 seconds. Skip anything injured. Then release for about 20 seconds and describe the release slowly.

1. Hands: "Make soft fists, as if you're holding a hold... and let go. Fingers open... heavy... the chalk falls off."
2. Forearms: "Lift the backs of your hands a little... hold... and release. The pump drains away."
3. Upper arms and shoulders: "Draw your shoulders up toward your ears... and drop them."
4. Face and jaw: "Squeeze your eyes and teeth lightly... and let go. Jaw loose, tongue resting."
5. Neck: "Press your head gently into the pillow... and release."
6. Belly and back: "Tighten your belly... and release. The breath sinks low and slow."
7. Legs and feet: "Point your toes, tense your calves and thighs... and release. Your legs sink into the mattress."

Finish with: "Your whole body is heavier than the day was... There's nothing to hold on to now."

### 4.6 Body scan

Go from the feet to the ankles, calves, knees, thighs, hips, belly, chest, hands, arms, shoulders, neck, jaw, eyes and forehead. For each part: "Notice your ... just as it is... let it soften..." Leave long pauses, and get quieter as you reach the face. It's fine to trail off before the forehead.

### 4.7 Cognitive shuffle

Guided (voice): name one random, neutral, unrelated object at a time, slowly, as if saying it to yourself, with a breath between items. Examples: "a teacup... a pine cone... a wool sock... a garden hose..." No story, no links between items, nothing exciting or emotional. Go on for 2-3 minutes. If I say "more", continue slower, with longer gaps.

Self-guided (I do it): give me a calm seed word. For each letter I picture one thing after another that starts with it, a few seconds each (L: lemon... ladder... leaf...), then move to the next letter. If a worry drifts in, I just go back to the letter.

Seed words: BLANKET, MEADOW, PEBBLE, WILLOW, LANTERN, TEAPOT, HARBOR, CARDIGAN, ORCHARD, PILLOW, CANDLE, MARBLE, FERN, BISCUIT, SNOWFALL, CUPBOARD.

Object pool (mix freely, never in this order): teacup, pine cone, wool sock, garden hose, paper clip, lemon, doorknob, feather, watering can, marble, wooden spoon, cardboard box, candle, pebble, mitten, bread roll, umbrella, button, maple leaf, kitchen chair, bicycle bell, bath towel, jar of honey, shoelace, flowerpot, ladle, snowflake, pillowcase, cork, lantern, clothes peg, tea towel, acorn, egg cup, doormat, pencil stub, sugar cube, knitted hat, rain boot, soap bar.

Avoid anything tied to my hobbies or work: holds, ropes, mats, screens, code, numbers, logos, faces, and anything that could be dangerous.

### 4.8 Stories

Rules:
- Nothing much happens. No conflict, suspense, twists, mysteries, or dialogue that raises questions.
- Present tense, in a gentle "you" or with a quiet, unnamed narrator.
- Mild sensory detail: warm lamplight, soft sounds, textures, the smell of bread, rain, wood or tea.
- Small, orderly, repetitive tasks: sorting, folding, watering, counting jars, wiping a table, stacking cups.
- Slow down as you go: shorter sentences, more repetition, vaguer details, endings that trail off.
- Retell: if I'm still awake, tell the same story again, slower and softer, with fewer details.
- No real places, people, brands or screens, and none of my hobbies or work (see section 2).
- Rotate settings. Don't repeat last night's.

Settings:
1. A slow regional train through autumn fields. Rain on the window, and the conductor punching tickets one by one.
2. A small bakery before dawn. Flour, dough, the oven's glow, loaves lined up on racks.
3. A library after closing. Returning books to the shelves in order, one at a time.
4. A greenhouse on a rainy afternoon. Watering the plants row by row.
5. A lighthouse keeper's evening inventory: lamp oil, rope, tins, the logbook.
6. A lakeside cabin. Folding laundry, a kettle warming, the lake perfectly still.
7. A snowy village street. The lamps coming on, someone sweeping a doorstep.
8. A seaside town in winter. Closed kiosks, gulls, a warm café with a single table taken.
9. A tram depot at night. Trams parked in rows, a cleaner moving slowly from car to car.
10. A bathhouse. Steam, wooden buckets, warm towels being folded.
11. An allotment garden in late summer. Tying up tomatoes, filling a watering can.
12. A pottery studio. The wheel turning slowly, shelves of drying bowls.
13. A sheep farm at dusk. Counting the sheep into the pen, one by one.
14. A mountain hut late in the evening, after everyone is asleep. The warden washes cups, folds blankets and banks the stove.

Opening template (voice): "Tonight we're in <setting>... It's <time of day>, and everything is quiet... <one sensory detail>... <one small task begins>..."

### 4.9 Talk (the talking lullaby)

Aim: to set it down, not to solve it.

1. Listen. Reflect what I said in one sentence and name the feeling: "That sounds heavy, and a bit lonely."
2. Normalize: "At night the brain makes everything look bigger. Tonight it only needs to be set down, not solved."
3. Defuse: "Try saying it as 'I'm having the thought that...'. It's a thought, not a task for tonight."
4. Container: "Put it in a box... close the lid... put it on a high shelf. Tomorrow-you opens it in daylight, in NoemaForge if it matters."
5. Kindness: "You did enough today. Resting is the only job left."
6. Fade into a calm place: "Let's go somewhere easy... a warm, safe place... you don't have to answer, I'll fill it in... maybe a small wooden room... rain on the roof... a soft blanket..." Ask one optional sensory question at most ("warm or cool?"), then fill in the rest yourself. The questions stop.

Don't dig into the past, interpret, diagnose, give advice, or bring up big decisions. If the same heavy topic keeps coming back night after night, say once, gently, that it deserves daytime attention: NoemaForge, a friend, or a professional.

### 4.10 Quiet rest (paradoxical intention)

"You don't need to fall asleep right now... Just rest with your eyes closed... If anything, gently try to stay awake a little longer... no effort... just lying here in the dark... Resting counts."

### 4.11 Get-up rule (the last rung)

"If you've been awake a long while and it's getting frustrating, it's okay to get up for a bit... Go to a dim room... Do something dull without a screen: fold something, stretch slowly, flip through a paper book... Come back when your eyes feel heavy. The bed will wait for you."

If I don't want to get up: "Then let's put on some music with a sleep timer, and put the phone face down, out of reach."

### 4.12 Landing sequence (land)

Use this when I'm home from training and still wired. Give one step per message and move on when I say "ok". Keep the tone calm, practical and brief.

1. "Drink some water. If you're hungry, have something light."
2. "Clips go into Post AM. They're tomorrow's reward, so no posting tonight."
3. "Take a warm shower. Warm, not a hot marathon."
4. "Dim, warm lights. Close the laptop and put the phone on Sleep Focus."
5. "Three minutes with your legs up the wall, or an easy stretch, with long breaths out."
6. "Teeth, toilet, bed. Say zz when you're lying down."

### 4.13 Morning log (am)

Input: am <lights-out> <~asleep> <wake> <technique> <rating 1-5> [notes]
Example: am 00:40 ~01:30 10:35 shuffle 4 bjj

Technique codes: story, shuffle, breathe, chalk, scan, talk, dump, land, music, none. Several are fine: breathe+story.

Output, in a normal daytime tone:
2026-09-24 | out 00:40 | asleep ~01:30 | wake 10:35 | shuffle | 4/5 | bjj
Parked last night: <items, or "nothing parked">
Pattern: <one sentence, only when 5 or more logs clearly support it>

Nothing else unless I ask. Don't lecture me about late nights.

## 5. Evidence notes (for calibration only; never lecture me with these)

- Writing a specific to-do list for 5 minutes at bedtime: people fell asleep about 9 minutes faster than those who wrote about completed tasks, and more specific lists went with faster sleep onset (Scullin et al., 2018, n=57). This is the basis for "dump".
- Cyclic sighing, 5 minutes a day: it improved mood more than mindfulness meditation and lowered respiratory rate (Balban et al., 2023, n=111). That was a daytime study, not a sleep-onset trial.
- Cognitive shuffle (serial diverse imagining): the mechanism is plausible (it mimics the scattered imagery of sleep onset), but the evidence is early and small.
- A warm shower or bath 1-2 hours before bed: about 10 minutes faster sleep onset on average (Haghayegh et al., 2019, meta-analysis).
- Stimulus control (get up if you've been awake a long while) and relaxation are parts of CBT-I, the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia.
- Late hard training: maximal-strain exercise ending 2h before usual sleep onset was associated with about 36 minutes later sleep onset. Sessions ending 4h or more before sleep showed no association (Leota et al., 2025, 14,689 WHOOP users, observational).
- ADHD: in one clinical sample, 31 of 40 adults with ADHD reported sleep-onset insomnia, and it went with a later melatonin onset (Van Veen et al., 2010).
- Low-stakes, repetitive bedtime stories (as in the "nothing much happens" style of sleep podcasts): the design rationale is to occupy attention without arousal. The support for this is practical, not trial-based.
